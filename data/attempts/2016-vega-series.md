Frank Vega has been arguably the most prolific claimant of the modern era,
posting a steady stream of papers on P versus NP from 2010 through the 2020s
across arXiv, HAL, Zenodo, TechRxiv, and small journals — initially arguing
P is not equal to NP (via undecidable problems inside NP, sparse complete
sets for coNP, and logarithmic-space verifiers), before flipping sides in
2025 with a claimed polynomial-time algorithm for SAT.

Representative entries in the series:

- *"Is P equal to NP?"* (2016):
  shows P = NP would imply P = EXP, hence P ≠ NP —
  [HAL hal-01270398](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-01270398)
- *"On P versus NP"* (2019):
  [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.2598772)
- *"Sparse complete sets for coNP: Solution of the P versus NP problem"* (2021):
  [Cambridge Open Engage](https://doi.org/10.33774/coe-2021-m6v73)
- *"Note for the P versus NP Problem"* (2024), his most-cited item:
  [IPIL](https://doi.org/10.59973/ipil.92)
- *"SAT in Polynomial Time: A Proof of P = NP"* (2025):
  [TechRxiv](https://doi.org/10.36227/techrxiv.173603344.46410054/v1)

None of these results has been accepted by the research community.
