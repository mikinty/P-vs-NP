In 1986/87 Ted Swart (University of Guelph) wrote a number of papers
(some of them had the title: *"P=NP"*) that gave linear programming
formulations of polynomial size for the Hamiltonian cycle problem.
Since linear programming is polynomially solvable and Hamiltonian cycle
is NP-hard, Swart deduced that P=NP.

In 1988, Mihalis Yannakakis closed the discussion with his paper
"Expressing combinatorial optimization problems by linear programs"
(Proceedings of STOC 1988, pp. 223-228).
Yannakakis proved that expressing the traveling salesman problem by a
symmetric linear program (as in Swart's approach) requires exponential
size.
The
[journal version](http://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(91)90024-Y) of this paper has been published in
Journal of Computer and System Sciences 43, 1991, pp. 441-466.
